\subsection{Interaction}

Attendees operate the system directly, using either the images provided or
their own photographs, through three scenarios of increasing analytical
depth.

\subsubsection{Scenario 1: dependence upon an individual cue.}
The attendee uploads a street-level photograph and suppresses a single
region --- a shopfront sign or a licence plate --- with a negative in-region
bias across all layers. A displacement of several thousand kilometres
identifies a cue upon which the model demonstrably depends; one of a few
kilometres indicates that it does not, irrespective of the prominence
saliency methods assign that region.

\subsubsection{Scenario 2: comparison across cue categories.}
Rather than delineating a region manually, the attendee selects a concept
from the detector's vocabulary; the corresponding detections are loaded as
the region and the same suppression is applied. Iterating over
\texttt{street sign}, \texttt{vegetation}, \texttt{architecture} and
\texttt{license plate} for one image orders the named cue categories by
their causal contribution to that prediction.

\subsubsection{Scenario 3: localisation of the effect within the network.}
The per-layer controls restrict the intervention to a chosen subset of the
$24$ layers. Depth proves decisive: the same bias applied to every layer
displaces the prediction by $2{,}042$~km on average, whereas restricting it
to the final layer displaces it by $320$~km and leaves four images in five
untouched (Table~\ref{tab:effect}). The intervention is reversible
throughout, resetting the biases to zero restoring the baseline exactly.

\subsection{Setup, Requirements and Relevance}

GeoProbe runs entirely on a single laptop (\TODO{GPU and memory}) with no
network connectivity: weights, GPS gallery, location embeddings, cached
detections and offline map tiles are all held locally. We require only table
space, mains power and, where available, an external monitor. The
demonstration addresses three concerns central to this community: multimodal
vision--language models, whose internal behaviour determines whether a
deployed system may be trusted; explainability of a causal rather than
correlational character, which multimedia forensics requires if attribution
claims are to withstand scrutiny; and image geolocation itself, whose dual
use renders an understanding of the evidence these models exploit
consequential beyond accuracy alone.
