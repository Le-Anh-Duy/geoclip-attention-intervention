\section{Conclusion}

We have presented GeoProbe, an interactive system that makes the question of
what a geolocation model uses amenable to intervention rather than inspection
alone. By biasing pre-softmax attention toward or away from selected regions,
independently per layer and at inference time, the system turns an arbitrary
photograph into a causal analysis whose outcome is read from the displacement
of the predicted coordinate. The same mechanism supports interactive
exploration and systematic measurement using prompt-free object proposals.
On Img2GPS3k, the multi-layer configuration selected on discovery did not
improve held-out geolocation error and did not significantly outperform a
single-layer intervention. At the same time, it displaced predictions by
$189.5$~km on average despite a feature cosine similarity of $0.9984$. This
distinction is central to GeoProbe: sensitivity is an explanatory measurement,
not evidence of improved prediction. Future work will replace global proposal
unions with learned sparse proposal selection and validate the resulting
policy on a fresh, untouched benchmark.
