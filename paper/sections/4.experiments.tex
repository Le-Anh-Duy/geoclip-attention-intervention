Regions are the class-agnostic object proposals retained at an objectness
threshold of $0.4$. For $947$ images no proposal survives, making every
intervened configuration an exact no-op; these are excluded, leaving
$N = 2{,}050$ images carrying $5{,}663$ proposals whose union marks on average
$87$ of the $256$ patches. Alongside mean and median top-$1$ geodesic error and
the conventional threshold accuracies we report the geodesic displacement
between the unmodified and the intervened prediction. The location encoder and
the gallery embeddings are computed once and reused, so the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:main} differ solely in the attention of the vision tower.

Three fixed interventions are evaluated: the union of an image's proposals
amplified uniformly across all $24$ layers with $a_\ell = 2$, $b_\ell = -2$;
a weaker one-sided bias, $a_\ell = 1$, $b_\ell = 0$, at the final layer alone,
isolating the read-out; and the same restricted to the single proposal of
highest objectness. Two further rows select, per image, the proposal minimising
the error, in the second case also admitting the unmodified prediction as a
candidate; these consult the ground truth and bound what region-conditioned
attention could achieve rather than describing a deployable configuration.

\subsection{Results}

Amplifying the proposed regions throughout the encoder degrades geolocation
substantially. Mean error rises from $1{,}829$ to $2{,}656$~km and median error
from $284$ to $673$~km, while accuracy within $25$~km falls from $31.3$ to
$23.4$~per cent. This is no small perturbation: the prediction moves by
$2{,}042$~km on average, and the movement is far from uniform in direction ---
$46.7$~per cent of images worsen, $25.5$~per cent improve and $27.8$~per cent
are unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:effect}). The regions are load-bearing, and the
magnitude of the degradation quantifies the extent of the dependence.

Depth is the decisive variable. The same amplification confined to the final
layer displaces the prediction by only $320$~km, leaves $80.7$~per cent of
images untouched and raises the mean error by $67$ rather than $827$~km.
Geographic evidence is accumulated across the depth of the encoder rather than
read off at the output --- precisely the distinction the per-layer controls of
Section~\ref{sec:demo} allow an attendee to establish for a single photograph.

Degradation under a fixed bias is not evidence that the regions are
uninformative. Selecting per image the proposal minimising the error reduces
the mean to $1{,}791$~km, and admitting the unmodified prediction as a further
candidate reduces it to $1{,}695$~km --- $134$~km below baseline, improving
$12.6$~per cent of images while harming none. A usable region-conditioned
signal exists that no global bias recovers, and identifying it image by image
is the analysis the interactive system supports.
