\begin{abstract}
Worldwide image geolocation models such as GeoCLIP estimate GPS coordinates
from a single photograph with high accuracy, yet they provide no account of
which image evidence supports a given prediction. Post-hoc saliency methods
address this question only correlationally: they indicate where activation
concentrates, without establishing that the highlighted evidence is what the
model relies upon. This paper presents GeoProbe, an interactive system for
causal analysis of image geolocation models. Given an image region ---
specified manually, or obtained automatically from an object detector ---
GeoProbe adds a key-indexed bias to the pre-softmax attention logits of the
CLIP vision tower, independently per layer, thereby increasing or decreasing
the attention mass allocated to that region. The system executes GeoCLIP
twice, without and with the intervention, and reports both predictions
together with the geodesic distance separating them. The magnitude of this
displacement is a direct causal measure of the region's contribution. On Img2GPS3k, amplifying the proposed regions
across all $24$ layers moves the prediction by $2{,}042$~km on average and
raises the mean error from $1{,}829$ to $2{,}656$~km, whereas confining the
same bias to the final layer moves it by only $320$~km: the regions are
load-bearing, and they become so through the depth of the encoder rather than
at the point of read-out.

\keywords{Image Geolocation \and Explainability \and Attention Intervention
\and Vision--Language Models.}
\end{abstract}
